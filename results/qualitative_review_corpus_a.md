It also says InvalidCiphertext is non-retryable by default, while Timeout is explicitly separated from DependencyUnavailable to support retry-budget and circuit breaker policy work.

For structured logging with stable field names, all KMS failure logs must include consistent fields such as kms.provider, kms.operation, kms.key_id, kms.key_arn when available, kms.region, kms.request_id, kms.error.kind, kms.error.code, kms.http_status, kms.retryable, kms.retry.count, and kms.latency_ms. Optional but recommended fields include kms.circuit_breaker.state, kms.timeout_budget_ms, kms.throttle, trace_id, and span_id. The implementation example in Go shows these exact stable names being attached by the wrapper logger helper.

Stable field names mattered because the system previously had multiple spellings for the same concepts, like kms_error, kms.err, kmsErrorCode, and aws_request_id, which forced on-call engineers to normalize logs by hand during timeout and throttling investigations. The change was needed so runbook queries stay consistent, alert conditions are reliable, dashboards can segment by provider, operation, and region, and responders can quickly tell whether an issue is AccessDenied, throttling, or invalid ciphertext.

If Decrypt fails, operators should rely on the canonical error kind together with the structured fields, especially kms.operation, kms.error.kind, kms.error.code, kms.request_id, kms.retryable, kms.retry.count, and kms.latency_ms. The context also adds redaction guardrails: never log plaintext, decrypted payloads, or DEK material, and kms.key_arn may be omitted and replaced with kms.key_arn_hash when configured. So together, the taxonomy plus stable logging fields give production-safe parsing, reliable JSON-shaped log consumption, and much faster triage across services.
